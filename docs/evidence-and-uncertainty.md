# Evidence and Uncertainty

Household knowledge is often incomplete.

Some facts are documented. Others are remembered. Some are inferred from context. Family members may disagree. Sometimes the correct answer is simply that the information is not known.

Household Knowledge Language preserves those distinctions explicitly.

## Epistemic states

### Known

Information treated as established within the record because available evidence supports it.

Example:

> A dated photograph shows the ceramic bird in the apartment in June 1991.

### Reported

Information attributed to a person or source.

Example:

> Elena recalls that her grandmother gave her the bird.

Reported information is not automatically converted into a known fact.

### Inferred

A conclusion derived from available evidence without direct confirmation.

Example:

> A river stone displayed beside a 1998 lake-trip photograph may have come from that trip.

### Disputed

Two or more materially conflicting accounts remain unresolved.

Example:

> One family member says the object was purchased in Michigan; another remembers it coming from Wisconsin.

A disputed record preserves both accounts rather than selecting one merely to create a cleaner story.

### Unknown

The information is not currently known.

Example:

> The exact year the object entered the family is unknown.

Unknown is a successful governed result. It is not a placeholder for something the system is expected to invent later.

## Evidence

Claims may be connected to evidence such as photographs, documents, messages, interviews, receipts, labels, physical inspections, and database records.

Evidence can also carry its own reliability and privacy classification.

A family interview and a dated receipt can both be useful evidence while supporting different levels of confidence.

## Claims and evidence are separate

HKL separates the claim from the evidence supporting it.

For example:

**Claim**

> The bird was displayed in the apartment by 1991.

**Evidence**

> A photograph dated June 1991 shows the bird on a shelf.

This separation makes it possible to inspect why a record says what it says.

## Evidence does not eliminate judgment

Evidence can be incomplete, ambiguous, or contradictory.

A photograph may establish that an object existed by a certain date without establishing when it was acquired.

An interview may preserve an important memory without independently proving every detail.

The purpose of the model is not to eliminate judgment. It is to make the basis for judgment visible.

## Privacy and evidence

The existence of evidence does not automatically make it public.

A record may acknowledge that a private letter exists while withholding its contents.

Evidence can therefore support a record while remaining restricted.

## Principle

The system should become no more certain than the evidence allows.

When uncertainty remains, preserving that uncertainty is more accurate than manufacturing a definitive answer.
