# Concept

Household Knowledge Language (HKL) is an evidence-aware model for preserving household knowledge.

Traditional household inventories are good at answering:

> What is this, and where is it?

HKL is designed to help answer a broader set of questions:

> Why does this matter?  
> Who gave it to us?  
> What changed over time?  
> What evidence supports the story?  
> What remains uncertain?  
> Who is allowed to see the answer?

## The core problem

Households contain more than objects. They contain gifts, inheritances, collections, photographs, documents, children's creations, ordinary tools, decorative items, and keepsakes that became meaningful because of the people and events around them.

A conventional inventory can preserve the existence of an object while losing why it was kept, who it came from, what event it represents, where it used to be, what other objects it belongs with, which details are remembered versus documented, which parts are disputed or unknown, and which information is private.

HKL treats those details as part of the record itself.

## A household record

A household record can describe a single object, a composite display, a grouped set, or a collection.

Each record may include identity, meaning, provenance, location, claims, evidence, relationships, events, privacy, and human approval.

The same structure can therefore represent both something simple and something relational.

For example:

- A ceramic bird may have one uncertain acquisition story.
- A memory shelf may contain several objects with different histories.
- An ornament collection may follow a year-by-year membership rule and still contain unresolved gaps.

## Meaning is structured data

HKL treats family meaning as something worth preserving alongside physical description.

A record may describe not only that an object is a blue ceramic bird, but also that the family keeps it because it is associated with a grandmother.

That meaning does not replace evidence. It is stored with provenance, attribution, uncertainty, and review state so emotional context and factual confidence can coexist without becoming the same thing.

## Uncertainty is part of the archive

A useful archive should not become more confident than its evidence.

HKL therefore allows records to preserve information as:

- Known
- Reported
- Inferred
- Disputed
- Unknown

An unresolved gap can remain unresolved.

The system is designed to preserve the difference between:

> No 2017 ornament is currently present.

and:

> A 2017 ornament never existed.

Those statements are not equivalent.

## Human authority

Software may help organize, validate, search, and retrieve household knowledge.

It should not independently declare family truth, erase disagreement, reduce privacy, or approve a record.

Human authority remains part of the model.

## Current public proof of concept

This repository currently demonstrates the model with three fictional examples:

1. A single sentimental object
2. A composite memory display
3. An annual collection

The examples are synthetic and exist only to demonstrate the public data model.
